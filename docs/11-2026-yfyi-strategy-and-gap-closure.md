# 11 — YFYI 2026 Strategy and Gap-Closure Addendum

Prepared: 19 August 2026  
Purpose: close the highest-value gaps in the research dossier and turn the findings into
a presentation-ready 2026 recommendation.

---

## Bottom line

YFYI 2026 should be positioned as:

> **Fikirden sahneye degil; fikirden ilk musteriye.**

The program has enough history, brand equity, mentor supply and capital adjacency. The
weakness is not legitimacy. The weakness is conversion: public evidence of pilots,
customers, investment, exports, incorporation and alumni outcomes is scattered or missing.

The 2026 edition should therefore move from a prize-centered accelerator to a
**venture-client and commercialization accelerator** for ODTU TEKNOKENT's strongest
company-building zones: deep tech, medtech, industrial AI, advanced materials, climate and
energy, defence/aerospace, biotech and B2B software infrastructure.

## What the evidence says

### YFYI's strongest assets

1. **Oldest/longest-running position**: 21 years, 5,500+ applications, 230+ startups,
   120+ mentors, 25+ countries per the current YFYI site.
2. **Institutional trust**: ODTU + ODTU TEKNOKENT + Elginkan continuity.
3. **Capital adjacency**: ODTU70, Growth Circuit, Collective Spark and INVEST 101.
4. **Deep technical source material**: ODTU labs, TEKNOKENT companies, sector clusters.
5. **Alumni flywheel**: V-Count, ISSD, Btech and others moved from participants to sponsors
   or ecosystem nodes.

### YFYI's weak spots

1. **Prize winners do not reliably become the best companies.**
   The status tracker shows several flagship winners have no traceable current operation,
   while stronger survivors often came from non-grand-prize or adjacent tracks.

2. **The award model is not indexed to startup reality.**
   A 100k TL prize was meaningful in 2012. It is not enough in 2026 to move a serious
   medtech, hardware, biotech, climate or defence startup through validation.

3. **Demo Day evidence is too pitch-heavy.**
   Public records emphasize winners and ceremony, not pilot conversion, customer proof,
   regulatory progress, revenue or follow-on capital.

4. **Outcome data is not productized.**
   There is no public alumni database. This makes the program harder to sell to sponsors,
   investors, applicants and international partners.

5. **Recent coverage has thinned.**
   2023-2025 information comes mostly from social posts and relay announcements, not
   structured press releases or official annual reports.

## 2026 strategy

### Strategic position

**YFYI26 should become ODTU TEKNOKENT's venture-client and commercialization pipeline for
technical startups.**

The promise:

> In 12 weeks, every selected team should leave with one of four hard outcomes: a signed
> pilot, a first paying customer, an investment committee-ready case, or a grant/regulatory
> path with named next milestones.

### Program architecture

| Track | Best for | Core outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Lab-to-Market | university IP, medtech, biotech, materials, sensors, defence, space | validation plan + prototype/pilot budget + IP/regulatory roadmap |
| Venture Client | B2B, industrial AI, climate, energy, mobility, SaaS infrastructure | signed pilot or paid proof-of-concept with sponsor/customer |
| Investment Readiness | incorporated startups with product/team | investor data room + partner fund screening + follow-on capital plan |
| Global Launch | startups ready for export or foreign incorporation | market-entry plan + international accelerator/customer/investor intros |

### Selection criteria

Prioritize evidence over presentation quality:

- technical defensibility
- customer pain and buyer clarity
- team capability
- realistic regulatory/manufacturing path
- capital efficiency
- export potential
- ability to run a pilot within 90-180 days

Deprioritize broad consumer marketplaces unless there is exceptional traction. The alumni
tracker suggests consumer/social/marketplace ideas have the weakest survival profile.

### Sponsor redesign

Every major sponsor should contribute one or more concrete instruments:

| Sponsor type | 2026 contribution |
|---|---|
| Elginkan | milestone grants, manufacturing/prototyping budget, "production-readiness" prize |
| ODTU TEKNOKENT | incubation office, lab access, data room support, alumni registry |
| ODTU70 / INVEST 101 / Growth Circuit / Collective Spark | investment office hours, IC screening, matched terms for top teams |
| OSTIM / Ostim Teknopark | prototyping, production partner matching, supplier access |
| Microsoft / cloud partners | cloud credits tied to technical architecture review |
| TEB / financial partners | founder finance, venture debt readiness, payment/accounting setup |
| Alumni partners | mentor pods, customer intros, angel checks, first-user feedback |
| Corporates/public agencies | paid pilots and procurement problem statements |

### Award redesign

Replace "one big cheque after the pitch" with four milestone awards:

1. **Prototype Award**: for measurable technical validation.
2. **Pilot Award**: paid customer or public-sector pilot budget.
3. **Production Award**: manufacturing, certification, clinical/regulatory, or IP support.
4. **Global Award**: foreign accelerator, trade fair, customer visit, or incorporation path.

Elginkan can remain the named flagship sponsor, but the instrument should reflect its
historical wording: support should be tied to moving toward production.

### Demo Day redesign

Each finalist should present a 5-slide evidence pack:

1. Problem and buyer.
2. Product and technical defensibility.
3. Validation: prototype, pilot, LOI, customer interview count, trial data or revenue.
4. Commercialization path: pricing, regulation, manufacturing, procurement, channel.
5. Ask: pilot, capital, partner, grant, lab, customer.

The jury should score evidence, not performance:

- 25% customer/pilot proof
- 20% technical defensibility
- 20% market and revenue path
- 15% team
- 10% capital plan
- 10% ecosystem fit

## Presentation structure

### Slide 1 — Title

**YFYI26: Fikirden Ilk Musteriye**  
Turning Turkey's longest-running early-stage accelerator into a commercialization engine.

### Slide 2 — What YFYI already owns

- 21 years
- 5,500+ applications
- 230+ startups
- 120+ mentors
- 25+ countries
- ODTU + ODTU TEKNOKENT + Elginkan continuity

### Slide 3 — The problem

YFYI has strong ceremony data, but weak public outcome data:

- partial winner lists
- no complete alumni registry
- sparse revenue/funding/job data
- several grand-prize winners not traceable
- non-grand-prize alumni often became stronger companies

### Slide 4 — What the alumni data shows

Tracked sample after gap search: 173 startups.

- 45 active
- 11 changed/pivoted/acquired/rebranded
- 41 passive
- 76 unverified

Key insight: deep tech, medtech, industrial and B2B infrastructure survive better than
consumer/social/marketplace startups.

### Slide 5 — Why 2026 must change

The 100k TL prize model is not enough for serious technical commercialization. The program
should keep the prestige of awards but make the real product:

- pilots
- customers
- milestone capital
- investment readiness
- export pathways

### Slide 6 — 2026 thesis

**YFYI26 = venture-client + commercialization accelerator.**

Every team leaves with one hard outcome:

- signed pilot
- first customer
- investor-ready case
- grant/regulatory/commercial milestone plan

### Slide 7 — Four tracks

- Lab-to-Market
- Venture Client
- Investment Readiness
- Global Launch

### Slide 8 — Sponsor model

Sponsors become builders of outcomes, not only award names:

- pilot buyers
- manufacturing partners
- cloud and architecture reviewers
- grant/regulatory advisors
- investor screeners
- alumni customers

### Slide 9 — New award architecture

- Prototype Award
- Pilot Award
- Production Award
- Global Award

Each is milestone-based and tied to a real next step.

### Slide 10 — Demo Day 2.0

No pitch theatre. Evidence-based judging:

- customer proof
- technical defensibility
- commercial path
- team
- capital plan
- ecosystem fit

### Slide 11 — Public impact system

Create the YFYI Alumni and Impact Registry:

- company status
- sector
- incorporation
- funding
- pilots
- jobs
- exports
- exits
- sponsor/alumni contributions

### Slide 12 — 2026 success metrics

By 6 months after Demo Day:

- 30-40 selected teams
- 15 signed pilots or paid PoCs
- 10 incorporated/newly active companies
- 8 investor-screened teams
- 5 follow-on investments or grants
- 3 international market-entry cases
- 100% public alumni/outcome tracking for the cohort

### Slide 13 — One-line close

**YFYI has already proved it can find ideas. YFYI26 should prove it can systematically
turn them into companies.**
