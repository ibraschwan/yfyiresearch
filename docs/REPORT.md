## Executive summary

**Yeni Fikirler Yeni İşler (YFYİ)** is the oldest continuously-running university startup
program in Turkey. ODTÜ (Middle East Technical University) and ODTÜ TEKNOKENT launched it
in **2005** as a student business-idea competition with **195 applicants** and a single
cash prize. Twenty-one editions later it is a multi-stage accelerator with a bootcamp,
a ten-week training and mentoring track, a December Demo Day, an award catalogue funded by
a dozen corporate partners, three venture funds attached to it, and an alumni network that
has itself become part of the sponsor base.

Five things define it:

1. **One sponsor has never left.** The **Elginkan Vakfı** has been the main sponsor from
   the first edition in 2005 through 2025 — 21 consecutive years. Its grand prize is the
   spine of the program: 50,000 TL (2007) → 75,000 TL (2008–2011) → **100,000 TL from 2012
   onwards**, paid in instalments tied to reaching production.

2. **It stopped being a competition in 2016.** ODTÜ TEKNOKENT's then-GM Mustafa İ. Kızıltaş
   said it explicitly: *"In 2005 we ran this as a student business-idea competition; today
   we ask how we can build a technology company that will operate worldwide. YFYİ is no
   longer a competition, it is an acceleration program."* The prize stayed, but it became
   the ending of a program rather than the point of one.

3. **It went global, hard, in 2017.** That year 50 teams went through a six-week
   pre-accelerator delivered by the **UC Berkeley Innovation Acceleration Group** using the
   Lean Launch method, and **seven teams spent seven weeks at Draper University** in San
   Francisco, pitching to Tim Draper. ODTÜ TEKNOKENT's own SF vehicles — **T-Jump** and
   then **Growth Circuit** — made this structural rather than ceremonial: Growth Circuit
   invested directly in seven 2017 startups and four 2018 startups.

4. **It is the front door to a much bigger machine.** YFYİ graduates flow into ODTÜ
   TEKNOKENT's incubation centre, CoZone co-working, T-BİGG (TÜBİTAK's 150,000 TL BİGG
   route), Growth Circuit, ODTÜ70 GSYF and a $6M early-stage fund created with the Türkiye
   Kalkınma Fonu. By 2017, ODTÜ TEKNOKENT hosted 330+ technology companies, **more than 60%
   of which were founded inside the technopark**.

5. **The alumni became the sponsors.** V-Count (2005) and ISSD (2010) went from
   contestants to award-funders and are now listed on yfyi.com as graduate business
   partners. Btech, which won the 2015 TEB Health grand prize, funded the Btech Prototyping
   Special Award in 2017. That loop closing is the strongest single evidence the program works.

**Headline claims (organiser figures):** 21 years · 5,500+ applications · 230+ startups ·
120+ mentors · alumni operating in 25+ countries · 200–250+ companies founded ·
1,000–2,000+ jobs created · $3M+ in seed capital deployed.

## 2. How it evolved — four eras

### Era 1 · 2005–2013 · The student competition
A screening funnel (applications → stages → ~100 hours of entrepreneurship and business-plan
training → jury final in November at ODTÜ's Culture and Congress Centre), a handful of
large cash prizes, and 2–3 years of free office space inside ODTÜ TEKNOKENT for winners.
Categories multiplied from one to five as defence, ICT, energy and health sponsors joined.

The 2013 funnel is the clearest picture of this era: **1,071 entrepreneurs with 777 business
ideas → 541 through stage 1 → 30 into training → 11 finalists.** About 1% of applicants
reached the final.

### Era 2 · 2014–2016 · Scale-up, segmentation, Silicon Valley
2014 brought Teknopark İstanbul as co-organiser, TEB as organisation main sponsor, a
~2 million TL total award package, and the **"ABD Kampı"** (US Camp) prizes — 25,000 TL
each from eight sponsors. 2015 split the program into **YFYİ IDEA** (pre-company students
and graduates ≤5 years out) and **YFYİ PRO** (tech companies aged 0–3, in four verticals),
and added Microsoft Azure and IBM Bluemix cloud-credit prizes worth $120,000 each. 2016
introduced the **T-IDEA / T-START / T-BOOST / T-BİGG** track architecture, String Ventures
bootcamps, and a four-week camp at T-Jump San Francisco.

### Era 3 · 2017–2019 · The globalised accelerator
The structural peak. UC Berkeley delivered the pre-accelerator; Draper University delivered
the accelerator; Growth Circuit delivered the money. Demo Day became a three-minute-pitch
event with a public vote (6,874 voters in 2017). In 2019 the format moved to a two-day
residential **"Inspiration Weekend"** in Sapanca, co-locating 24 startups with partners and
investors for 48 hours.

### Era 4 · 2020–2025 · International partners, December Demo Days
The 2020 cohort ran with Estonia's **Startup Wise Guys**: 40 teams admitted → 10 weeks of
training and mentoring → 27 completed → 19 pitched at an online Demo Day on 2 April 2021.
ODTÜ TEKNOKENT introduced its own **Internationalisation Award**, funding winners to attend
a foreign accelerator of their choosing. From 2022 the Demo Day settled into December, and
the investment layer thickened: ODTÜ70 GSYF, Growth Circuit, Collective Spark, a $6M fund
with the Türkiye Kalkınma Fonu. In 2025 a parallel **KALTEV YFYİ** edition ran at ODTÜ's
Northern Cyprus Campus.

## 3. How a cohort runs today

```
APPLICATION  →  SELECTION  →  BOOT CAMP  →  TRAINING & MENTORING  →  DEMO DAY  →  AFTER
via yfyi.com    screening      intensive     ~10 weeks,               pitch to      free office at
+ JotForm       + jury         kickoff       120+ mentor pool,        jury +        ODTÜ TEKNOKENT
                                             partner/investor         investors     incubation centre;
Two profiles:   ~40 teams                    intros                                 seed capital;
• idea stage    (2020)                                               cash +         internationalisation
• 0–4yr tech                                                         in-kind        award; alumni
  company                                                            awards         network for life
```

**Eligibility** has been stable since 2015:
- **Idea track** — undergrad/master's/PhD students, or graduates up to 5 years out, no
  company yet. Teams meeting TÜBİTAK BİGG criteria route into **150,000 TL** of support.
- **Company track** — technology companies aged 0–3 (2015) or 0–4 (2016+), historically
  capped at those having raised under 100,000 TL.

**Application windows** have drifted: March–May (2013–2018) → 5–22 August (2019) →
September–October (2023–2025). **Demo Day** moved November → October → December.

## 6. Money: who funds it and how the prizes are shaped

**The constant:** Elginkan Vakfı, main sponsor since 2005. It runs a wider university
competition portfolio (Boğaziçi 2008–14, İTÜ Çekirdek 2011–14, YTÜ 2013–14) — YFYİ is the
oldest strand and the only one still alive.

**Corporate sponsors by era.** Defence and telecoms dominated the early years (Savunma
Sanayii Müsteşarlığı 2008–18, Türk Telekom 2011–12, Turkcell 2013, Aselsan 2012–14,
DenizBank 2012–13, Intel 2011–15). Industrials and banks joined mid-decade (General
Electric 2014, Arçelik 2014–16, Microsoft 2014→, TEB 2014→, Teknopark İstanbul 2014–15,
IBM 2015). The current era is media, logistics and venture (Onedio 2016→, KoçSistem
2017–22, Tüpraş 2018–19, EPDK–ELDER 2018–19, PTT/PTTAVM/PTTEM 2019→, VLMedia 2022→,
Invest101 2024, Collective Spark and ODTÜ70 GSYF as investment partners). OSTİM and its
technopark and fund have been present since 2011 — the second-longest continuous supporter
after Elginkan.

**Prize scale by era.**

| Era | Grand prize | Special awards | Advertised total pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2007 | 50,000 TL | — | — |
| 2008–2011 | 75,000 TL | 10,000–25,000 TL | — |
| 2012–2013 | 100,000 TL × 3 grands | 25,000 TL | 400,000 TL cash / 1.5M TL total |
| 2014–2015 | 100,000 TL × 4 grands | 25,000 TL US camps + $120k cloud credits | ~2M TL |
| 2016–2018 | 100,000 TL | 15,000–50,000 TL | — |
| 2019–2022 | 50,000–100,000 TL (often split) | 20,000–50,000 TL | — |

**Non-cash instruments matter as much as the cash:** US Camps at T-Jump SF (2–4 weeks),
Draper University Hero Training (7 weeks), Elginkan and ODTÜ TEKNOKENT internationalisation
awards, free incubation office (1–3 years), Onedio advertising support up to 50,000 TL,
Microsoft/IBM cloud credits, KoçSistem technology packages, PTT logistics discounts,
Btech prototyping, and direct investment from Growth Circuit / ODTÜ70 GSYF / Collective Spark.

**An important caveat:** the flagship prize has been nominally **flat at 100,000 TL since
2012** and has been *split between two winners* in 2019, 2021 and 2022. In real terms the
Elginkan grand prize is worth a small fraction of what it was worth in 2012. Anyone
analysing the program's generosity over time must deflate.

## 8. Assessment

**What the program does unusually well.**

- **Longevity and sponsor continuity.** Twenty-one years with the same anchor sponsor is
  rare anywhere. It gives YFYİ a credibility and a mentor pool that newer Turkish programs
  cannot buy.
- **Deep-tech bias.** The alumni list is heavy with biotech, materials, medical devices,
  defence and energy hardware — the categories most accelerators avoid because they are
  slow. Payment in instalments tied to reaching production is an unusually well-designed
  instrument for exactly that kind of company.
- **A real international leg, when it is funded.** The 2017–2018 UC Berkeley → Draper →
  Growth Circuit pipeline was a genuine structural advantage, not a photo opportunity.
- **The alumni flywheel closes.** V-Count, ISSD and Btech went from contestants to funders.

**Where it is exposed.**

- **The prize has not been indexed.** 100,000 TL has meant progressively less every year
  since 2012, and has been split more often recently. The award's signalling value now
  likely exceeds its financial value.
- **Public visibility has declined.** Turkish tech press covered YFYİ closely from 2013 to
  2021; since then, coverage has thinned to the program's own social channels. For a
  program whose main non-cash asset is legitimacy, that matters.
- **No public alumni database or outcome tracking.** The "230+ startups" claim cannot be
  audited by anyone outside the organisation, and the application-count figures published
  over the years do not reconcile with each other.
- **The Silicon Valley leg is no longer visible.** Draper and UC Berkeley disappear from the
  record after 2018; Startup Wise Guys appears once, in 2020. The internationalisation
  award (cash toward an accelerator of the winner's choosing) is a lighter-touch substitute.

**What would strengthen the record.** A published alumni registry with cohort year, current
status and capital raised; a single consistent applications metric; and inflation-indexed
prizes.
